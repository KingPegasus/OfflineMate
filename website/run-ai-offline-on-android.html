How to run AI offline on Android

You can run an AI model directly on an Android phone with no internet connection. You need enough RAM, some free storage, and an app that runs the model on your device. Here’s what that involves.

What “offline AI” means

Most AI assistants send your message to a server, which generates the reply and sends it back. Offline AI skips that trip: a language model is stored on your phone, and your phone’s own processor generates the reply. Your message doesn’t need to leave the device, and airplane mode doesn’t stop it working.

What your phone needs

RAM matters most. The model has to fit in memory while it runs. OfflineMate’s smallest tier targets phones with 3–4 GB of RAM, and its largest targets 10–12 GB or more.

Free storage. Model files are large. Expect about 430 MB–1.3 GB for the Lite tier, 2.5 GB for Standard, and 2.6 GB for Full.

An Android version the app supports. OfflineMate runs on Android 7.0 and later.

To find your RAM, open Settings and look under About phone. The exact location varies by manufacturer.

How to set it up with OfflineMate

  1. Install the app

    Install OfflineMate from Google Play. It’s free and needs no account. You can also build it from source.

  2. Pick a tier and model

    Choose the tier that matches your RAM. Each has a default model. Compare the models if you want a different one.

  3. Download on Wi-Fi

    Setup downloads the chat model along with speech and search files. Wi-Fi is the better choice for files this large.

  4. Turn on airplane mode and test it

    Ask a question. If a reply appears, the model is running entirely on your phone.

Downloading or changing modelsYes

The trade-offs compared with cloud AI

You gain privacy and independence. Replies are generated on your phone, there’s no account, and a dropped connection doesn’t stop anything.

You give up some capability. A model small enough for a phone is less capable than a large cloud model, and it can get things wrong. Check anything important.

Your phone does the work. Generating replies is demanding, so a long session can use noticeable battery, and speed depends on your hardware.

Answers can be out of date. A model only knows what it learned before release. Web search can fill the gap when you’re online.
